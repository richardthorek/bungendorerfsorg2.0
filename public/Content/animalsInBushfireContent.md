### Animals in a Bushfire

This district has a lot of horse and livestock properties, and animals need their own plan — you can't always load them at the last minute.

**Plan early, especially for horses and livestock:**

- Decide now whether you will relocate animals early or leave them in a cleared paddock — trying to float horses out once a fire is close is often too dangerous and too slow.
- If relocating, identify a safer property or agistment well outside the district and arrange it before fire season, not on the day.
- If leaving animals on the property, choose the largest, most cleared paddock available, away from sheds, fences with flammable material, and tree lines. Open gates between paddocks so animals aren't trapped against a fence line.
- Make sure animals are permanently identifiable (brand, microchip, tag) in case you're separated from them.
- Have enough water available that doesn't rely on mains power (a generator or pump can fail with the power).
- Keep a list of your animals, a recent photo of each, and your vet's contact details with your bushfire survival plan.

**Small animals and pets** should be part of your household evacuation plan — have carriers ready and practice loading them before you need to do it in a hurry.

More detailed guidance: [NSW RFS — Animals and Pets in a Bush Fire Survival Plan](https://www.rfs.nsw.gov.au/plan-and-prepare/develop-a-bush-fire-survival-plan/animals-in-your-survival-plan)
