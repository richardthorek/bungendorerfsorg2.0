### Permits &amp; Burning, Plain English

Thinking about a burn-off? Here's what actually applies, without the jargon.

- **Bush Fire Danger Period (BFDP):** a fixed season (usually **1 October – 31 March**, but the RFS Commissioner can vary it — check the Controlled Burn cell above for today's status) during which you need a **fire permit** to burn anything in the open, even a small pile. Outside the BFDP, you don't need a permit, but you still need to notify.
- **Getting a permit is free.** Apply through the RFS permit system (see Quick Links) — a local Fire Control Officer assesses conditions before issuing it.
- **Always notify first, permit or not.** At least **24 hours before you light up**, notify the RFS _and_ your neighbours — even outside the Bush Fire Danger Period. This isn't optional paperwork: it's what stops an unannounced burn being reported as a wildfire, and what gives your neighbours a chance to say if smoke will be a problem for them.
- **In this district, a permit can be suspended on a High-or-above Fire Danger Rating day**, even if you already have one — check the Controlled Burn cell above before you light anything, every time, on the day.
- **Total Fire Ban means no burning at all**, permit or not, any time of year — including cooking fires, incinerators and most machinery that can throw a spark.
- **Keep pile sizes sensible and manageable** — a pile you can't put out quickly with the water and people on hand is too big.

**Notify a burn or apply for a permit:** see the Quick Links, or the "Permits" link in the top utility bar.
