### Leaving Early Is the Safest Choice

**The single most important decision in a bushfire is when to leave — and the safest time is always before the fire arrives.** Homes and roads are most dangerous in the hours just before and during a fire front. Waiting to "see how bad it gets" is how people get caught. If you're not staying to actively defend a fire-ready property, plan to leave the night before or early on a bad-conditions day — not when you can already see smoke.

### Set Your Trigger Now, Not on the Day

Decide today what will make you leave, while you're calm and can think clearly — not in the middle of a fire when it's hardest to think straight. A trigger might be:

- The Fire Danger Rating for Southern Ranges reaches **Extreme** or **Catastrophic**.
- A **Watch and Act** or **Emergency Warning** is issued for our area.
- You see smoke, smell smoke strongly, or the sky turns orange/brown.
- The Kings Highway is your only way out and it's still open.

Write your trigger down and agree it with everyone in your household, including kids and anyone who needs extra time or help to leave.

### What To Do, By Fire Danger Rating

| Rating       | What it means                                                 | What to do today                                                                                                                |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Moderate     | Most fires can be controlled.                                 | Plan and prepare — check your property, refresh your bushfire survival plan.                                                    |
| High         | Fires can be dangerous.                                       | Be ready to act. Decide what you'll do if a fire starts nearby.                                                                 |
| Extreme      | Fires will spread quickly and be extremely dangerous.         | Take action now. If your property isn't fire-ready to the highest level, go to a safer location well before a fire threatens.   |
| Catastrophic | If a fire starts and takes hold, lives are likely to be lost. | **Leave bushfire risk areas.** Homes cannot withstand fires in these conditions — go early, in the morning or the night before. |

### A Property Checklist You Can Do This Weekend

- Clear leaves and debris from gutters, decks and around the base of the house.
- Mow grass short and remove dead vegetation within a few metres of buildings.
- Move firewood piles, gas bottles and anything flammable away from the house.
- Check hoses reach all sides of the house and taps/pumps actually work.
- Know where your water, gas and power shutoffs are.
- Keep a battery radio, torch, first aid kit and copies of important documents ready to grab.

### If You've Left It Too Late

If a fire is threatening and you haven't left, sheltering at a **Neighbourhood Safer Place** is a last resort — not a plan. See "Where To Go If You've Left It Too Late" below for our nearest one.
